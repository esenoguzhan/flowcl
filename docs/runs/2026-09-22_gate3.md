## 1. What this gate answers

Spec §10.3 Gate 3:

> Does the new task need protected directions?  
> **`c_l` after T2; `c_l ≈ 1` ⇒ hard projection incompatible with plasticity.**

§7.3: `c_l = ‖G_l M_l M_lᵀ‖_F / ‖G_l‖_F`, with the full-gradient denominator and the projection
on the **input** dimension of `nn.Linear.weight`. `c_l²` is the share of the layer's gradient
energy that hard projection (GPM) would delete.

**Reading of "c_l after T2"** (a deviation, noted as §11 requires). What is measured is **Task-2
gradients at the start of T2**, taken at the T1 checkpoint. These are the gradients GPM would
project from T2's first step. No Task-2 training happens in this gate.

Choices fixed **before** the run (`flowcl/analysis/gates.py`):

| Choice | Value |
|---|---|
| Blocked layer | mean per-batch `c_l ≥ 0.95` at ε = 0.95, meaning projection leaves ≤ 31% of the gradient norm |
| Verdict | FAIL if a strict majority of the **trunk** (49 layers) **or** of the **decoder** (42 layers) is blocked |
| Deciding gradient | per-batch (64, the training batch size) Object gradients, averaged over one pass: 114 batches, 7 281 samples |
| Point vs CI | the point estimate decides; a layer whose bootstrap CI straddles 0.95 is flagged `borderline` |
| Evidence only | ε = 0.90 / 0.99; energy-weighted and full-dataset `c_l`; `√ρ_l` isotropic RMS baseline; T1 control; ΔW; replicate |

## 4. What the numbers say

**`c_l` is high everywhere, not only in the decoder.** Across the network, 87% of Object's gradient
energy at the T1 checkpoint lies in Spatial's ε = 0.95 input subspace. The gate passes because 0.95
was chosen as the meaning of "≈ 1", and most layers sit at 0.90–0.95. The substantive result is the
level itself, not the pass.

**Trunk: high overlap, modestly above chance.** `c_l ≈ 0.94` against an isotropic RMS baseline of
about 0.65–0.70 (`c/√ρ ≈ 1.3–1.4`). T1's trunk subspace already covers ~45% of each layer (Gate 2),
so much of T2's overlap is simply what a large subspace captures. Overlap is flat across depth:
median per block is 0.935–0.951.

**Decoder: overlap far above chance, concentrated early.** Self-attn and MLP inputs put 89% (0.946²)
and 86% (0.927²) of T2's gradient energy into the **2–6%** of input dimensions that T1 occupies.
That is 4.8× and 5.6× the isotropic baseline. This supports Gate 2 §5's second reading: the
decoder's dominant input directions are **shared across tasks** (what an action chunk is), not
specific to one task. Overlap falls with depth: median per block is 0.961, 0.926, 0.875 and 0.866
for blocks 0 to 3. The least-overlapping layers are in block 3 (self-attn Q/V and cross-attn Q at
0.761). This is the part of the decoder where hard projection leaves most room.

**Aggregates agree.** Mean, energy-weighted and full-dataset `c_l` differ by at most 0.033
(`flow_head.blocks.2.mlp.fc1`: 0.887 / 0.887 / 0.920). The verdict does not depend on how batches
are aggregated.

A plausible mechanism, *not tested here*: activations are uncentered (GPM convention, Gate 2 §8), so
the mean activation direction carries a large share of every layer's input energy and of every
task's gradient. A per-rank curve `c_l(k)` would show how much of `c_l` the first few directions
carry. It can be computed from the stored bases (Gate 2 §12) with no new measurement machinery.

## 5. Why the pass is fragile, and what that does and does not mean

- **Threshold location.** 33/49 trunk layers (and 40/49 on the replicate) lie in [0.90, 0.95).
  Moving the blocked cut to 0.90, or the basis to ε = 0.99, flips the verdict.
- **Not noise.** CIs are about 0.001 wide and only 4 layers are borderline. A second seed would
  probably move individual layers across 0.95; the replicate moved the trunk count from 16 to 7 with
  a median |Δc| of only 0.010.
- **What `c_l` does not say.** `c_l` measures how much gradient *norm* projection removes, not
  whether the *remaining* ~36% points anywhere useful. With Adam, which rescales small residual
  gradients, a heavily projected layer can still learn if the orthogonal remainder carries task
  information. Whether GPM can actually fit Object is an empirical question for §10 step 8, and this
  gate cannot answer it.

The gate is not re-decided here. The criterion was fixed before the run and it passed. What changes
is how later results must be read (§7).

## 6. Controls and evidence

### 6.1 T1 self-gradient control (Spatial data, same checkpoint and basis)

| | all | trunk | decoder |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `c_global` T1 control | 0.928 | **0.965** | 0.917 |
| `c_global` T2 | 0.935 | 0.940 | **0.933** |

- **Trunk behaves as expected:** T1's own residual gradients lie more inside T1's basis than T2's do
  (0.965 against 0.940).
- **Decoder is reversed:** 19 of 42 decoder layers have T1 `c_l` < T2 `c_l` (1 trunk layer does). As
  decided in the plan, these are marked **for inspection, not as a basis error**. T1 is converged:
  its gradient norms are about **1.6%** of T2's (median ratio 0.016), so they are residual noise.
  Meanwhile T2's large gradient is dominated by the shared, high-energy directions. That is the same
  story as §4, seen from the other side.

### 6.2 Realized seq_ft update ΔW = W(stage 1) − W(stage 0)

| ε | all | trunk | decoder |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.90 | 0.691 | 0.757 | 0.461 |
| **0.95** | **0.786** | **0.853** | **0.556** |
| 0.99 | 0.906 | 0.959 | 0.735 |

What seq_ft *actually* changed while learning Object, and while destroying Spatial (Gate 1: 90% →
0%), went through T1's directions much less than the raw start-of-T2 gradients did, especially in
the decoder (0.556 against 0.933). Two plausible causes, not separated here:
- Adam's per-coordinate scaling does not preserve subspaces.
- The gradient direction changes over 30 000 steps as Object is learned.

Weight decay is negligible (lr·wd·steps ≈ 3·10⁻⁴). Two readings for the thesis:
1. A large part of the forgetting-causing update was **orthogonal** to T1's input subspace, above
   all in the decoder. Hard projection would leave that part untouched, so GPM cannot be assumed to
   prevent forgetting in full.
2. The raw-gradient `c_l` overstates how much of the realized update runs through protected
   directions. GPM projects the gradient **before** Adam, and Adam's rescaling can move the update
   back into the protected subspace. That is a known limitation of combining GPM with Adam, and step
   8 must address it: project the update after Adam, or use SGD.

## 7. Consequences for later steps

1. **GPM (step 8) is at risk on plasticity.** At ε = 0.95 it removes ~87% of T2's gradient energy,
   and at ε = 0.99 ~97%. Report GPM's **just-after-training diagonal** (`R[j][j]`) as a primary
   number next to forgetting. A GPM run that "does not forget" because it never learns T2 must not
   read as a success.
2. **Fix GPM's ε before its first run.** Record 0.95 as the default, as in §7.2, and report the
   sweep. At ε = 0.99 the whole trunk is blocked. Do not pick ε after seeing GPM's results.
3. **SGP's `α_l` is the natural response** to `c_l` this high: scale, don't delete. The per-layer
   pattern here (decoder block 0 most shared, block 3 least) is a principled prior for `α_l`, but it
   must be set before seeing SGP's results.
4. **Adam + projection:** see §6.2, reading 2. Step 8's `ProjectedOptimizer` must say where in the
   Adam update the projection acts, and its test must cover it.
5. **Gate 4** (`ρ_l(s)`, `c_l(s)`) becomes more interesting. If the shared decoder directions are
   flow-time structure, `s`-binned bases should split them, which is the thesis claim. Trunk and
   cross-attn K/V cannot show `s`-dependence by construction (Gate 2 §13.4).

3. **No `c_l(s)`.** Gate 4.
4. **No evidence on whether projected training still learns T2.** `c_l` is a gradient-geometry

   vs chunk and flow-time structure).
6. **Adam vs projection.** §6.2 shows the realized update differs sharply from the raw gradient; the
   cause is not separated.
7. **Only `seq_hetero`.** `seq_correlated` (same motion, different objects) should show higher `c_l`
   still, but it is gated on the language check, which has not been run.
