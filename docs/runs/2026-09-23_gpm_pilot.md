## 1. What the pilot answers

Gate 3 left a training question open. Hard projection at ε = 0.95 removes ~87% of Object's gradient
energy (`c_global = 0.935`). Can the policy still learn Object while keeping Spatial?

| Arm | Trainable | Projection |
|---|---|---|
| `gpm_projected_adam` | registry weights only (41 954 304) | gradient before AdamW **and** AdamW's realized update after the step, against the fixed T1 memory |
| `freeze_only` | the same 41 954 304 | none |
| *reference:* seq_ft stage 1 | all 53 813 767 | none |

- **Naming.** `gpm_projected_adam` is stricter than canonical GPM (Saha et al. 2021 use SGD).
  Throughout this record it is **not** called plain "GPM".
- **Frozen parameters.** Both arms freeze 11 859 463 parameters (146 tensors): the §7.4 list plus
  the encoder projections, context queries and positional embedding.
- **Recipe.** The Gate 1 recipe, read from that run's `config.yaml`, with a fresh AdamW at T2 as in
  Gate 1.
- **Pairing.** Both arms use seq_ft stage 1's exact data, `s` and `A_0` stream. Rollouts use seq_ft's
  seed namespace. Episode seeds were **verified identical** across the GPM arm, the freeze arm, and
  seq_ft's own stage 0 and stage 1 evaluations.

**Pre-registered criteria**, with references read from the Gate 1 artifacts:
- Object ≥ 78 − 15 = **63%**; Spatial ≥ 90 − 15 = **75%**.
- The point estimate decides; a cell whose CI straddles its threshold is flagged `borderline`.

## 2. Decision

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Pre-registered outcome for `gpm_projected_adam` | **Hard projection is viable.** Object 88.0% ≥ 63%, Spatial 80.0% ≥ 75%. |
| Is the outcome robust? | **Object yes, Spatial no.** Spatial 80.0 [68.0, 90.0] straddles 75% (borderline). A second seed could land below it. |
| Does projection cause the retention? | **Yes, entirely.** GPM − freeze_only on Spatial is **+80 pp [+68, +90]**. freeze_only keeps nothing (0/50). |
| Does projection cost plasticity? | **No measurable cost.** GPM − freeze_only on Object is **+10 pp [−2, +22]**, and loss curves converge to the same level (§5). |
| Is T1 fully preserved? | **No.** Spatial is 80% against 90% just after T1 (−10 pp [−24, +4], paired; not significant). The Spatial probe loss rose 4.5× (0.0099 → 0.0444). |
| Does the §7.4 freezing matter by itself? | **No.** freeze_only equals seq_ft on both tasks (Spatial 0 = 0; Object +0 pp [−14, +14]). |
| Was the applied update actually orthogonal? | **Yes.** Total displacement `c_global = 7.1e-6`, against 0.776 for freeze_only. The per-step residual was at worst 2.4% of its bound. `action_in` was exactly frozen. |
| Was Gate 3's pessimism wrong? | **It was about norm, not usefulness** (Gate 3 §5). The raw gradient kept `c_l ≈ 0.93` for all 30 000 steps, and the ~36% of the norm left over was enough (§4). |

## 4. Mechanism: what the projection actually did

Every 100 steps, the method logged the median over layers of the raw-gradient overlap `c_l`, and of
the overlap of AdamW's step with the protected subspace before the update projection:

| Step | raw gradient `c_l`, trunk | raw gradient `c_l`, decoder | AdamW step `c_l`, trunk | AdamW step `c_l`, decoder |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.947 | 0.889 | — | — |
| 100 | 0.927 | 0.893 | 0.192 | 0.162 |
| 1 000 | 0.926 | 0.877 | 0.151 | 0.130 |
| 5 000 | 0.933 | 0.909 | 0.093 | 0.086 |
| 15 000 | 0.942 | 0.963 | 0.073 | 0.084 |
| 25 000 | 0.933 | 0.956 | 0.070 | 0.090 |

1. **Gate 3's number held for the whole run, and did not block learning.** Object's raw gradient
   pointed ~93% (by norm) into T1's protected subspace from the first step to the last. Every step
   discarded ~85% of the gradient energy, yet Object reached 88%. The protected component is large
   but not needed to learn Object. This is consistent with Gate 3 §4's reading that the shared
   high-energy directions are not task-specific. It is still a hypothesis.
2. **Projecting the gradient alone would have leaked.** Even with the gradient projected, AdamW's
   step put 16–19% of its norm (2.6–3.6% of its energy) into protected directions early on, decaying
   to 7–9% (step-0 values are undefined because no step existed yet). The post-step projection
   removed this. Whether that leak changes forgetting is untested (a `gpm_grad_only` ablation).
3. At step 29 900 the AdamW-step overlap rises in the trunk (0.458). The learning rate is 2.7e-9
   there, so it is irrelevant for the result. A plausible cause, not verified: near convergence,
   AdamW's decoupled weight decay (∝ W) dominates the step, and W has large components along T1's
   input directions.

**Numerical guarantees, checked during the run:**
- The residual `‖D M‖ ≤ 1e-6‖W‖ + 1e-3‖D‖` was enforced on every step for all 91 layers. At worst it
  reached 2.4% of the bound (`trunk.blocks.6.mlp.fc2`); the median was 1.5%.
- The total displacement over 30 000 steps has `c_global = 7.1e-6` (trunk 8.1e-6, decoder 4.4e-6).
- `action_in` (full-rank basis, so its projector is exactly zero) moved exactly 0. Every other layer
  moved.
- All frozen tensors were bit-identical to stage 0 in both arms.

## 5. Plasticity: learning speed and quality

Mean training loss by window. Both arms saw the same batch stream.

| Window (steps) | gpm_projected_adam | freeze_only |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0–100 | 0.685 | 0.511 |
| 100–500 | 0.096 | 0.082 |
| 500–2 000 | 0.048 | 0.047 |
| 2 000–10 000 | 0.025 | 0.027 |
| 10 000–20 000 | 0.014 | 0.016 |
| 20 000–30 000 | 0.0090 | 0.0087 |

- Projection slows the first few hundred steps: the first 50-step window averaging below 0.05 comes
  at step 879 against 858.
- From step 500 onwards the curves are indistinguishable.
- Final and last-50 losses: 0.00765 / 0.00854 for GPM, 0.00759 for seq_ft, 0.00806 (last-50) for
  freeze_only.

Rollout quality on Object:
- GPM succeeds on 44/50, with a median horizon of 134 steps (seq_ft 137, freeze_only 137).
- Paired against seq_ft on the 34 episodes both solve, GPM is 12 steps faster on average (median −3).
- The episode-level overlap with seq_ft is 34 both / 5 seq_ft-only / 10 GPM-only. The +10 pp is not
  significant.

## 6. Stability: where the remaining Spatial loss comes from

Paired against stage 0, episode by episode:
- 36 episodes succeed in both.
- 9 succeed only at stage 0: 11, 13, 21, 22, 26, 33, 38, 39, 48.
- **4 succeed only under GPM**: 5, 27, 46 and 49. These were four of stage 0's five failures.

That much churn in both directions says the retained Spatial skill is **perturbed**, not partially
erased: marginal episodes flip either way. The skill is also slightly slower. On the 36 shared
successes the median horizon rises by +3 steps (mean +7.7), with one slow success at 328 steps.

**Why is there any loss when the update is exactly orthogonal?** Two known approximations in GPM
remain, and this run cannot separate them:
1. **ε = 0.95 leaves 5% of each layer's T1 input energy unprotected**, and the update is free to act
   on it.
2. **Stale bases (compounding).** The bases describe each layer's T1 inputs *at stage 0*. As earlier
   layers change, even orthogonally for their own inputs, the T1 inputs to later layers drift away
   from their stored subspaces. Over 8 trunk and 4 decoder blocks this compounds.

Rollout noise also plays a part: −10 pp [−24, +4] includes zero.

The probe loss, 4.5× stage 0 but 16× below seq_ft, is the cleaner measure of the residual
interference.

## 8. Protocol deviations and caveats (§11)

1. **Dirty provenance.** Both run registries and `pilot.json` record
   `f01026cf8d3b7142da4cc1205a97dbc7ed06d793-dirty`. The pilot implementation was uncommitted. The
   working tree is unchanged since the run, so committing it now gives a SHA containing exactly the
   code that ran. That commit should be named in this record once made.
2. **The sanity run was skipped.** There is no `sanity.json`. Its assertions are covered by the
   full run itself:
   - orthogonality was enforced every step;
   - the frozen-tensor check passed;
   - `action_in` stayed exactly at 0;
   - losses were finite;
   - the Object loss fell from 1.12 (first 50) to 0.0085 (last 50);
   - the Object probe fell from 1.62 to 0.0072.

   The timing question it was meant to answer is answered directly by §7.
3. **Not canonical GPM**: AdamW with gradient and update projection (§1, §4).
4. **Frozen protocol.** Both arms freeze the 11.86M non-registry parameters. freeze_only shows this
   neither helps retention nor hurts plasticity here, but it is a difference from seq_ft that the
   Stage A table must list.
5. **Fixed memory.** Only the T1 basis was used. GPM's incremental accumulation (paper Eq. 8–9, §7.2)
   was not exercised. With 4 tasks the free subspace shrinks. Gate 2 found the trunk ~45% occupied
   after T1 at ε = 0.95, so T3/T4 plasticity is the open risk.

## 9. What this run does *not* show

1. **One seed.** Spatial is borderline, so the viability verdict needs seed 1 and seed 2 before it
   carries weight.
2. **Two tasks.** No T3/T4 and no accumulated memory, so capacity saturation is untested.
3. **Which of the two approximations in §6 causes the residual forgetting.**
4. **Whether projecting AdamW's update matters for forgetting**, as opposed to guaranteeing
   orthogonality. That needs a `gpm_grad_only` ablation.
5. **Anything about SGP, the `s`-binned method, or ε ≠ 0.95.**
6. **Whether the high `c_l` is the uncentered mean direction.** The `c_l(k)` curves suggested in
   Gate 3 §14 are still not computed.

## 10. Consequences for the build

- **GPM is a real contender for the Stage A table, not a predicted plasticity failure.** Gate 3's
  risk (§7.1 there) did not materialize for T1→T2.
- **Step 8 can reuse this code.** `flowcl/methods/gpm.py` already has the projection, the allowlist,
  the per-step orthogonality check and the post-step hook. Missing are:
  - the incremental memory update (Eq. 8–9), with a test on synthetic nested subspaces;
  - the `ProjectedOptimizer` packaging and `c_l` logging required by §7.3;
  - running inside `run_continual` over all four tasks.
- **Report plasticity next to forgetting.** Keep `R[j][j]` as a primary number: here it was
  88% ≥ 78%, but accumulation could change that.
- **Resolve the evaluation seed pairing before Stage A.** `continual_run_id` includes the method
  name. This pilot shows how valuable pairing is: 13 discordant Spatial episodes would otherwise be
  indistinguishable from seed noise.

**Cheap follow-ups, no new training:**
1. Per-layer T1 *interference activation* `‖ΔW x₁‖ / ‖W x₁‖` (paper §7, Fig. 3), computed two ways:
   - with stage-0 inputs, which measures the direct ε residual;
   - with the GPM checkpoint's own T1 inputs, which measures the compounded drift.

   This separates the two causes in §6.
2. `c_l(k)` curves from the stored bases (Gate 3 §14).

**Next training runs, in order of information per GPU-hour:**
1. **Seed 1 of the same pilot.** Needs seq_ft seed 1 stage 0 (~43 min + ~10 min eval) and Gate 2
   bases (~2 min), then the two arms (~2 h). It decides whether "viable" survives the Spatial
   borderline.
2. **`gpm_grad_only`** (~1 h). Does projecting AdamW's update matter?

## 11. Suggested thesis wording (draft)

> To test whether hard gradient projection remains compatible with learning the new task despite
> the high gradient overlap measured at Gate 3, we trained the second `seq_hetero` task (Object)
> from the first-task checkpoint under GPM-style projection against the first task's ε = 0.95 input
> bases. The gradient was projected before AdamW, and AdamW's realized update was projected again
> after each step. All non-projectable parameters were frozen. A control with identical freezing
> but no projection forgot the first task completely (0/50 rollouts, as did unconstrained
> fine-tuning), while the projected policy retained 80.0% [68.0, 90.0] against 90.0% [80.0, 98.0]
> just after the first task (paired difference −10 pp [−24, +4]) and learned the second task to
> 88.0% [78.0, 96.0] (control 78.0%). The new task's raw gradients kept 93% of their norm in the
> protected subspace throughout training, so hard projection discarded most of every gradient
> without a measurable loss of plasticity. This was a single seed and a single task pair; the
> retention estimate lies within noise of the pre-specified 75% criterion.

Do not shorten this to "GPM prevents forgetting". It reduced T1's probe-loss increase by 95% and
kept T1 rollouts within a non-significant 10 pp. Whether it holds across seeds and four tasks is
open.
